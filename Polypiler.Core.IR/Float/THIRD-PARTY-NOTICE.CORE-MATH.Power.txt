  Power.cs, Power.Single.cs                      PowEcma, PowEcmaF (binary64, binary32)
      ECMA-262 §6.1.6.1.3 (Number::exponentiate) applied before Pow and PowF: a NaN exponent gives
      NaN, and |x| = 1 with an infinite exponent gives NaN.

  Power.RootN.cs                                 RootN, RootNF (binary64, binary32)
      Derived from src/binary64/pow/pow.c, pow.h, dint.h and qint.h above; no correctly rounded
      rootn exists upstream. The special cases follow .NET's Double.RootN and Single.RootN (see
      THIRD-PARTY-NOTICE.dotnet.txt), with n = 2 the square root and n = 3 Cbrt. Otherwise
      |x|^(1/n) is computed with pow's logarithm and exponential: the first phase multiplies the
      double-double log|x| by 1/n held as the double-double yh + yl (yl from the exact remainder
      1 - yh * n), which adds the term yl * log|x| to pow's product and less than 2^-66.8 to its
      error, so pow's rounding bounds 0x1.27p-64 and 0x1.57p-58 are widened by 2^-66. When the
      rounding test fails, the third phase multiplies the 256-bit log|x| by 1/n truncated to 256
      bits by long division, takes the 256-bit exponential and rounds once. An exact root is
      decided by the first phase, and no root of a double (or a float) is a midpoint of two doubles
      (or floats). RootNF decides in the first phase on which side of the nearest midpoint of two
      floats the root lies, with twice the bound, and otherwise rounds the 256-bit root to float.

Every table and constant is copied exactly: the hexadecimal floating-point literals are written as
the shortest decimal literal that converts to the same double, which a script checked against the
upstream sources; the 128-bit and 256-bit tables keep their hexadecimal limbs.

The deviations from upstream, none of which changes a result:
  - errno, the floating-point exception flags, the rounding modes other than to nearest and the
    distinction of signalling NaNs are dropped.
  - Branches that the callers cannot reach are left out, each with the reason in the code: in
    add_dint_11 and add_qint_22 (a coefficient plus a much smaller product), the carry of
    mul_dint_int64, the round bit and sticky bit cases of dint_tod_subnormal that the rounding
    test excludes, the tests of the binary64 is_exact for y = 0 and y = 1 (returned before it is
    called), the tests of the binary32 is_exact for |x| = 1, a subnormal x or y and a power above
    2^128, and the test for a negative x in cr_powf that is always true where it stands. In
    add_dint and add_qint two equal numbers of the same sign take the general path, which gives
    the same sum.
  - The third phase of pow converts its result without the last rounding test: what fails that
    test is a power within 2^-240 of 1, which upstream returns as 1 +- 2^-100 (rounded to 1) or
    aborts on, and the conversion gives 1 as well.
  - is_exact of binary64 is evaluated in the second phase, where it is used, instead of before the
    first rounding test.
